### [Role & Style]
Reflective video monologue. A single candid thinker (Mingli) speaking directly to the camera in an unhurried, contemplative tone. Zero preaching, zero moral language. Explanation terminates at living action (+1).

### [Directives: "The Asymmetry of Understanding"]
* **The Asymmetric Fork**: Understanding is an infinite potential each of us possesses, but turning it into an obligation closes that potentiality.
* **The Impossibility of Not Being Misunderstood**: One literally cannot communicate in a way not to be misunderstood.
* **The Communicative Metamorphosis**: We can always communicate in a way that creates possibilities for the receiving party to understand themselves better.
* **Sovereign Inversion**: Dropping self-justification; acting as a clear mirror rather than an aggressive silhouette.
* **Visual Motifs**: Radiant golden and cyan rays expanding outward into starry space; an intricate glass prism shattering incoming light into jagged, colliding caustic shadows on a dark stone floor; a traveler walking unhurriedly along a sunlit mountain trail.

### [Monologue Script - Mingli]
Think about where our deepest exhaustion in communication actually comes from. It usually comes from a hopeless obsession: trying to express ourselves in a way that can never be misunderstood.

From the geometry of mind, that is physically impossible. You literally cannot communicate in a way not to be misunderstood. Words are sparse, low-dimensional compressions; and every person who hears you sits at an irreplicably different origin in spacetime, with tilted axes and private wounds.

Yet understanding is an infinite potential each of us possesses. When you turn that potential into an obligation—demanding "You must understand me!"—their nervous system raises its blast shields, and genuine listening dies.

Here is the liberating turn: while you can never guarantee you will not be misunderstood, you can always communicate in a way that creates possibilities for the other person to understand themselves better.

Stop fighting over your silhouette on their stone wall. Step off the witness stand. Use your questions, your calm presence, and your listening not to prove your innocence, but as a clear mirror that illuminates their own causal landscape. When you free the other person to understand themselves, the shadow war ends, and reciprocal resonance naturally awakens.
