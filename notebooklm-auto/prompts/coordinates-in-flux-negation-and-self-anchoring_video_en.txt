### [Monologue Script - Mingli]
In public conversations, it is common to see critiques claiming that denigrating others is merely a low-cost bid for superiority.

What rarely gets noticed is the self-referential trap built into the proposition itself: classifying someone else's behavior as 'low-cost' is already an act of seizing intellectual high ground.

From there, reactions split into two familiar patterns: either pointing fingers outward, declaring that society is full of such people; or engaging in retrospective purging, admitting past guilt to prove how enlightened the present self has become.

Both impulses share a single subconscious premise: treating reality as a rigid, closed container of finite volume. In an imaginary zero-sum space, consciousness believes it must rely on negation to secure standing—either by expelling the other or by aggressively erasing its own past iterations.

Yet the world begins in Mind, ends in Mind, and never departs from Mind. What we perceive as the other is an internal projection within our own cognitive landscape. Every act of denigration cuts a blind spot into our own perceptual map; negating the other is directly an act of inward self-reduction.

Within the vast orthogonal degrees of freedom of open reality, things simply differ. There is no global scalar hierarchy. The illusion of superiority is merely the self-destructive price of crushing multidimensional existence onto a single 1D line.

Inward reflection is not a punitive courtroom; it is the living metabolic engine of consciousness (+1). Awareness is an ongoing river that does not need to shatter earlier molds to justify its present flow.

When we release the compulsion to seize ground through negation and scalar ranking, we anchor our coordinate with ease—grounded in real-time context, free from rigid pedestals, and navigating open reality with unbounded degrees of freedom.
