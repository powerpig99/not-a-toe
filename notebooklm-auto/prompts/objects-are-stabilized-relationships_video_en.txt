### [Directives: "Objects Are Stabilized Relationships"]
* **The Core Paradox**: Quantum mechanics shows that relations like symmetry and entanglement precede particles. Common sense says objects are primary. Some philosophers throw up their hands and say we need Deleuzian mysticism to understand reality.
* **The Simple Razor**: "Objects are stabilized relationships." An object is not a freestanding metaphysical substance. It is a cluster of relations that has achieved dynamic invariance and causal stability under physical friction and conservation laws.
* **Nouns as Scaffolding**: We invent nouns—"stone," "chair," "currency"—not to discover eternal essences, but to chunk dense networks of stabilized relations so our finite minds don't freeze up with information overload. Reification is mistaking the noun for an uncaused essence.
* **First-Person Ground (+1)**: Relations don't float in an abstract third-person void. Reality is fundamentally first-person perspective reality; the "physical" is its stabilized macroscopic symptom. You don't need Deleuzian jargon to know what a boulder is: you push it, it resists, and you bear the energetic cost (+1).
* **Visual Motifs**: Holding a solid geometric stone while amber lines of light show the relational forces holding it together; an architectural bridge standing firm against rushing water; a thinker setting the stone down and walking forward.

### [Monologue Script - Mingli]
We grow up assuming that objects are the basic furniture of reality, and that relationships are just secondary connections between them. First there is the billiard ball, then there is the collision.

Then quantum mechanics comes along and inverts everything. In Relational Quantum Mechanics, properties don't even exist until systems interact. Relations precede objects. But recently, a philosopher argued that because this breaks our classical logic, we have to abandon everyday language and retreat into the weird, obscure philosophy of Gilles Deleuze.

You don't need Deleuzian mysticism to understand quantum reality. The resolution is right in front of us: objects are stabilized relationships.

A stone on the trail isn't an uncaused metaphysical substance. It's a dense cluster of electromagnetic bonds, nuclear forces, and gravity that has achieved structural stability. It resists external perturbation. When you kick it, the ground pushes back, the lattice holds, and you feel the physical friction.

We invent nouns—"stone," "table," "atom"—as cognitive scaffolding. They’re computational shortcuts to keep our minds from being overwhelmed by infinite microscopic relations. The mistake is forgetting that the noun was just a label, and imagining an independent, observerless substance sitting in a vacuum of nowhere.

Reality is fundamentally first-person perspective only. The physical world is its stabilized macrosymptom. When you understand that objects are stabilized relationships, you no longer worship rigid institutions or static idols, but you never slip into nihilism either. You know that changing any relationship requires paying the real physical price, taking the next irreversible step into the living world (+1).
