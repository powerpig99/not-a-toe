Why do minor disagreements in everyday life so quickly escalate into defensive hostility?

Because we subconsciously cling to the illusion of a shared objective coordinate system—believing that facts sit inside a single neutral container, and that anyone failing to see what we see must be irrational or hostile.

In causal reality, that "view from nowhere" is a complete fiction.

There is no transcendent coordinate frame outside of mind. Each conscious awareness is not merely a vector inside a static room, but the living generator actively spanning the coordinate axes of that room.

When you look at another person, you never access an unmediated external object. What you perceive is an inward orthogonal projection—a scalar dot product of their signal cast upon your own intentional axis. You only ever see the shadow that fits your current ruler.

When perspectives diverge and the dot product turns negative, the naive reflex is to treat that negative reading as an alien invader, fighting desperately for standing on a narrow 1D track. But the adversary you are attacking is an internal projection generated by your own measurement frame. The entire conflict is a self-inflicted civil war of cognitive dissipation.

Orthogonality is not indifference—it is a complementary, unexplored dimension of reality. Genuine synchronization is not forced conformity, but two sovereign generators freely aligning their directional axes.

The true freedom of mind is knowing you are the living generator spanning dimensions at every single moment—holding your own compass, honoring orthogonal freedom, and navigating an open continuum.
