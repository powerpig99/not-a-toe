### [Directives for This Episode: "The Continuum is a Modeling Convenience"]
* **Core Tension**: Calculus relies on treating the non-zero increment dx as zero to force closure. Even inside pure math, Weierstrass proved that continuity does NOT equal smoothness—most continuous curves are jagged, nowhere-differentiable fractals!
* **The "Quarantine" Pattern & Collective Amnesia**: Whenever the fundamental limits of formal closure are exposed (Weierstrass's "monsters", Gödel's incompleteness, the Liar's / Russell's paradox), the establishment labels them "pathological exceptions," puts up a warning fence, and turns around acting as if nothing happened. Yuhan must challenge this psychological evasion: "Why are we so eager to exile every crack in the foundation as an 'exception'? What fantasy are we defending?"
* **The Epistemological Pivot**: Mingli reveals that continuity is not an external fabric, but the mind's real-time attempt to render the completed step (t-1) and the open next step (t+1) coherent with each other. Reflection, imagination, and reasoning are successive tempos of the same reflexive fold.
* **Visual Metaphors to Emphasize**:
  - **The Weierstrass Fractal Zoom**: A seemingly smooth curve under a microscope that resolves into infinite, sharp jagged peaks at every level of magnification—proving that zooming in never flattens out into a line.
  - **The "Monsters" Gallery**: A Victorian museum hall of formal logic and mathematics with grand classical statues, where Weierstrass's curve, Gödel's paper, and the Liar's sentence are locked away in a dark curtained cabinet labeled "Pathological Curiosities," while scholars outside continue worshiping frictionless blueprints.
  - **The Pleated Paper Fold**: A heavy paper strip folding in three rhythms: tight accordion pleat (Reflection), expansive diagonal wing (Imagination), and crisp faceted crease (Reasoning), split by a razor-thin golden light seam (`dx ≠ 0`).
  - **Premise Amnesia**: Freezing the folded paper on a pedestal, throwing a cloth over the hands that made it, and claiming it is an eternal, timeless axiom.

### [Intro Script - Mingli]
In calculus, the increment dx is never actually zero—treating it as zero is just a modeling convenience to make the math close. When Weierstrass showed that a curve can be continuous everywhere but differentiable nowhere, mathematicians called it a "monster" and tried to quarantine it. It’s the exact same trick we see with Gödel and the Liar’s Paradox: treating structural limitations as mere "exceptions" so we can keep pretending the world is closed and smooth. Today, we look at why reality refuses to be smoothed out, and how reflection, imagination, and reasoning are different tempos of the mind folding upon itself. Let us begin.
