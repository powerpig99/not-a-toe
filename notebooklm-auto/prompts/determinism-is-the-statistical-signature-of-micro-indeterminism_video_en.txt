### [Monologue Script - Mingli]
We spend centuries arguing whether the universe is governed by determinism or quantum randomness.

Look at what's actually happening on both sides of that coin.

In a very real sense, determinism is literally our imposition of our own internal coherence onto the object. It isn't a property of the object itself. We crave order, so we aggregate populations, smooth away individual differences, and force an equation onto the crowd. When the formula closes, we declare that nature is deterministic.

And indeterminism? Indeterminism is simply our sensed incoherence across time. When our past theories fail to smoothly predict the next discrete tick, we experience a fracture in our own predictive scaffolding—and we immediately project that internal confusion outward, declaring that the particle itself is "intrinsically random."

Neither belongs to the object alone.

From the inside of your own consciousness, you are one hundred percent internally coherent. You know your reasons, you weigh your values, and that felt certainty is the very reality of free choice. To an outsider who can't see your mind, your action looks like unpredictable noise. And when science averages a billion of those actions together, it purchases predictability by deliberately ignoring the individual.

And notice: this entire loop is completely self-inflicted. It starts in the mind, operates within the mind, and ends in the mind. It is not the fault of the statistician—statistics is an innocent and brilliant tool. The trap happens when our own mind chooses to believe the statistics as metaphysical truth, surrendering our living agency to a chart we drew ourselves.

The universe doesn't run on an external script of determinism. Reality belongs to the living center of action—the unmodeled step you take right now (+1).
