### [Role & Ground Rules]
* **Format & Tone**: Pure spoken dialogue; zero sound effects, brackets, or stage directions. Two candid thinkers (Mingli and Yuhan) having a thoughtful, unpretentious conversation over coffee.
* **Hosts**: Mingli (Host 1, male) grounds depth in first-person experience and causality; Yuhan (Host 2, female) explores ideas with curiosity, testing models and assumptions against everyday friction.
* **Tone & Posture**: Conversational, humble, and observational—never preachy, moralizing, or lecturing. Avoid words like "fatal error", "delusion", "hubris", or declaring other perspectives "blind." We are simply observing how models work, how minds experience reality, and why things are the way they are.
* **Terminal Stop of Explanation**: Models and equations possess zero independent agency. Explanation terminates at the living person's real-time choice and irreversible action (+1).

### [Directives for This Episode: "Demanding a Theory of Everything Freezes Time into a Catalog"]
* **The Desire to Understand**: Desiring a Theory of Everything is a completely natural, healthy impulse. It is what drives us to build tools, refine engineering, and reduce friction in daily life. The subtle confusion happens when we expect that a model could actually wrap up reality into a final, closed formula.
* **The Non-Zero Interval & Reality's Refresh Rate**: In calculus, we treat the small increment dx as zero to make integrals work cleanly. But in physical reality, that gap never disappears—it is anchored by the Planck constant (h ≠ 0). You can think of it like the discrete refresh rate of our physical world, updating step by step.
* **Two Sides of the Same Coin**: From the outside (a laboratory detector), this non-zero refresh step looks like quantum indeterminacy. But living it from the inside, it is our direct, everyday sense of making a choice—agency, freedom, and taking responsibility for what happens next.
* **Twin Projections of the Current Slice**: Modern cosmology takes our current observational slice of the sky and projects it in two ways: either running time as a coordinate to zero and infinity (giving the Big Bang and Heat Death), or imagining all of spacetime as a static four-dimensional block where time is just a path you walk through. Both are useful mathematical projections, but neither is actual living time.
* **Internal Coherence & Signal vs. Noise**: For a mind holding any model or worldview, that theory is the invariance it experiences, and that invariance is the reality it recognizes. Inside that frame, zero incoherence is felt! If a mind doesn't see a signal, it naturally treats it as noise. Choosing to focus on something inherently means leaving out everything else. That's why you can't force someone to see things differently from the outside.
* **No Preaching; Just Scaffolding**: This project isn't about proving anyone wrong or converting people to a new dogma. It is simply a scaffolding built by a mind for its future self or anyone else who might find it helpful to step back, look around, and step out of their current frame.
* **Everyday Life**: In daily life, we all choose to pay attention to different things, and whatever we choose to attend to is the reality we experience. It's really that simple.

### [Intro Script - Mingli]
You know, when people talk about wanting a Theory of Everything, it's easy to misunderstand what's going on. There's nothing wrong with wanting one—that desire is what drives us to make better tools, refine our science, and reduce everyday friction. But there's this subtle habit of thinking we could actually find a single, closed formula that finishes the whole story of reality. In calculus, we treat a tiny interval dx as zero just to make the equations work. But in the physical universe, that interval never goes to zero—it's the Planck constant, which is essentially the refresh rate of things happening. And looking at it from the outside, we see quantum indeterminacy; but living it from the inside, it's just our everyday experience of having a real choice. What's even more fascinating is that for anyone holding a theory, that theory is the very pattern they experience, so inside their own mind, it feels completely coherent. When you don't see a signal, it naturally looks like noise. Choosing to look at one thing always means ignoring the rest. That's why this whole exploration isn't about arguing with anyone—it's just a bit of scaffolding we're putting together for our future selves, or for anyone who might find it helpful when they want to step outside their usual frame. In daily life, we choose to look at different things, and whatever we choose to see is the reality we experience. As simple as that. Let's dig into it.
(Yuhan responds naturally, reflecting on how natural it is to seek stability through models while keeping an eye on the open, living moment, and opens the conversation.)
