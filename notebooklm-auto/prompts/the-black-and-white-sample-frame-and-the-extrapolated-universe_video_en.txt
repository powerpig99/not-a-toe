* **The Retrospective Givenness Illusion**: The world of an infant and an adult are completely different. Yet whenever our perception expands to encompass a larger horizon, the mind instinctively assumes it was always already there waiting—manufacturing the illusion that "the universe is a pre-existing static container."
* **The Living Movie & Adjacent Frames**: Reality is an irreversible, continuous HDR movie advancing frame by frame (+1). Physical measurement requires a finite exposure window. Physics collects the most adjacent frames of the movie and averages them out into a single composite slide.
* **The Closed Universe Assumption**: Physics treats this time-averaged slide as an isolated, self-contained closed universe, desaturating it into an abstract black-and-white grid.
* **Why Local Forecasts Succeed**: Because this invariance genuinely holds across immediate adjacent frames, applying the model to near-term local forecasts (eclipses, orbital mechanics, GPS timing) is surprisingly and undeniably accurate.
* **The Overfitting Extrapolation**: Intoxicated by this local predictive success, theorists mistake a local high-order fit over adjacent frames for an eternal cosmic law. They extrapolate the slide backward to a Big Bang singularity and forward to Heat Death.

* **The Measuring Mind**: Everything begins, remains, and concludes within the mind. The cosmos is not an unrolled film strip; it is an open causal calculation advanced in the now.

### [Monologue Script - Mingli]
Modern physics presents a staggering story: the universe was born in a Big Bang, will die in Heat Death, and sits frozen inside a four-dimensional block where your entire life is already etched in stone. But notice the deep psychological trick beneath it. An infant's world extends barely past the edge of a crib. As we grow, our world expands continuously. But human psychology harbors a profound blind spot: the moment our perception expands to encompass a larger world, we immediately assume that larger world was always already there, waiting like a completed stage set. We mistake the expanding boundary of our own perception for a pre-existing, static container. Theoretical physics repeats this exact psychological reflex. Because any measurement takes time, physics collects the most adjacent frames of the living movie. Over that tiny window, the scene looks stable. Physics averages those frames together into a single composite frame, treats that average as an isolated closed universe, and desaturates it into a black-and-white grid. Because that invariance genuinely holds across immediate adjacent frames, when you use this model for local forecasts—predicting an eclipse or synchronizing a satellite clock—it is surprisingly accurate. But physicists make a fatal category jump. Intoxicated by that local success, they take this local high-order fit and extrapolate it across infinity. Reversing the equations squeezes all pixels into a dot—they call it the Big Bang. Fast-forwarding the equations dilutes the pixels to void—they call it Heat Death. Extruding the slide into a cylinder, they call it the Block Universe. Even the fundamental constants are just the technical specifications of that sample frame: the Planck constant is pixel size, light speed is the calibration ruler, and spacetime curvature is the Mercator distortion of flattening the cosmic horizon. We are measuring the universe with our own mind. The universe is not an extruded film strip. It is an unceasing, living reality advancing frame by frame in the present act.
