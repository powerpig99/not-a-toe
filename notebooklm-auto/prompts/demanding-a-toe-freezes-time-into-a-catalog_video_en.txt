* **The Natural Desire for Certainty**: Frame the pursuit of a Theory of Everything not as a moral or intellectual failure, but as a completely natural human longing to understand the world and reduce daily friction. The subtle shift happens when we forget that models are tools, and imagine that a closed algebraic catalog of reality could actually be achieved.
* **The Non-Zero Refresh Rate**: Calculus sets `dx = 0` as a clever modeling convenience to make continuous equations work. But physical reality anchors that non-zero gap in the Planck constant (`h ≠ 0`)—the discrete refresh rate of things actually happening.
* **The Two Faces of the Refresh Gap**: What laboratory instruments measure from the outside as "quantum indeterminacy" is the exact same gap experienced from the inside as our everyday sense of living freedom, choice, and responsibility.
* **The Two Projections of the Current Slice**: How theoretical cosmology extracts our current observational slice of the universe and projects it two ways: either extrapolating time as an independent variable to zero and infinity (producing the Big Bang and Heat Death), or locking everything into a static 4D block where time is just a line traversed across a fixed cube. Both are geometric projections of a dead model, while actual time remains the unfreezable living step.
* **Internal Coherence & Signal as Noise**: Within any mind holding a theory, zero incoherence is felt. That theory is the invariance it experiences, and that invariance is the reality it recognizes. If a mind doesn't see a signal, that signal is naturally brushed off as noise. Choosing to see one thing inherently means ignoring everything else.
* **The Video as Scaffolding, Not Preaching**: Make it crystal clear that this exploration is not trying to lecture or convert anyone. It is simply a scaffolding built by a living mind for its future self or anyone else who might find it useful to step outside their current conceptual container and look at reality afresh.
* **Everyday Reality**: In daily life, we all choose to see different things, and whatever we choose to see is whatever reality we experience. As simple as that.

### [Monologue Script - Mingli]
When we talk about wanting a Theory of Everything, it's very easy to misunderstand what's actually happening. There's nothing wrong with wanting a complete theory. That desire is just what drives us to build better tools, refine our science, and reduce the friction we run into every day. 

The subtle thing is how easily we start believing that reality itself could actually be finished—wrapped up into a final, closed formula. In calculus, we treat a tiny interval as zero just to make the math work out smoothly. But in the physical universe, that gap never collapses to zero. It's anchored right there in the Planck constant—the discrete, frame-by-frame refresh rate of the physical cosmos. 

And look at what that means: what a physics experiment measures from the outside as quantum indeterminacy is the exact same thing we feel from the inside as our everyday sense of freedom—the simple, real ability to make a choice and step into the next moment.

Even the way we model the universe shows this. We take our current view of the sky and project it two ways: either we run time all the way back to the Big Bang and forward to Heat Death, or we freeze everything into a four-dimensional block where past, present, and future are treated like static museum displays. Both are mathematical projections, but neither is actual living time.

And there's an even simpler truth about how our minds work: whenever someone holds a theory or a worldview, that theory is the pattern they actually experience. Inside their own mind, it feels completely coherent. When we can't see a signal, we naturally brush it off as noise. Choosing to focus on one thing always means leaving everything else in the background. That's why you can't argue anyone out of their world from the outside. 

This whole project isn't about lecturing anyone or proving a point. It's just a bit of scaffolding—something built in the present for a future self, or for anyone else who might occasionally want a quiet foothold to step back, look around, and see reality as it is. In everyday life, we all choose to see different things, and whatever we choose to see is whatever reality we experience. It really is as simple as that.
