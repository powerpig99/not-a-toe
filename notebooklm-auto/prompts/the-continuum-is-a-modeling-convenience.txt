### [Directives for This Episode: "The Continuum is a Modeling Convenience"]
* **Core Tension**: Calculus relies on the increment dx never being zero, yet treating it as zero when small enough to make equations close. This mathematical convenience is routinely mistaken for an ontological discovery about reality, generating the illusion of "continuity."
* **Specific Pitfall to Avoid**: Do NOT get bogged down in formal math debates or academic jargon (no lecturing on epsilon-delta proofs). * **The Epistemological Pivot**: Mingli shows that continuity is not an external substrate, but the mind's real-time effort to render the completed step (t-1) and the open next step (t+1) coherent. Reflection, imagination, and reasoning are not separate mental organs, but successive tempos of the same reflexive fold.
* **Premise Amnesia**: Formal systems take the frozen output of a fold, forget the time and friction it took, and parade it as an eternal axiom. Logical paradoxes are just the neglected dx asserting that it refuses to be zero.

It’s an ingenious modeling trick—but we made the mistake of thinking reality itself is smooth and continuous. Today’s piece explores what happens when we stop pretending that interval is zero, and how reflection, imagination, and reasoning are really just different tempos of the mind folding back upon itself. Let us begin.
