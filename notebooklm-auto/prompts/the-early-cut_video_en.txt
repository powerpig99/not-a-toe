### [Monologue Script - Mingli]
When someone builds an ambitious unification theory like Chronoflux, it's worth seeing where it gets things right. Treating inertia as a delay in re-rendering rather than a mysterious property is a real step toward mechanism. And Herbert is completely right that a mathematical limit isn't just a pile of discrete steps. But the first cut arrives too early. He begins with a conserved temporal current, treating time as a fluid. That substitutes transport for causality. Continuity isn't an actual fluid flowing through nature; it's simply the coherence that causality has once a discrete step has happened and we've declared the interval negligible. In calculus, we set dx to zero for modeling convenience; in physics, that interval is real. And the irony is that in defending a continuous world, when he classified a critic's tool as inadequate, that classification was itself a sharp, discrete cut. Reality took a step forward anyway. You can't freeze time into a finished catalog, because the act of writing the catalog is already part of the world moving on.
