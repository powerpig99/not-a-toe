Zero preaching, zero moral language.

### [Core Directives: "Determinism is the Statistical Signature of Micro-Indeterminism"]
* **Internal Coherence as Felt Agency**: From the first-person perspective, we are 100% internally coherent. We know our reasons, our perceptions, and our values. This felt determinism is not a mechanical cage; it is the very sense of control that constitutes freedom of choice.
* **The External View as Noise**: From the outside, an observer cannot access our internal deliberation. To an outside instrument, an unobservable free variable registers as pure unpredictability—stochastic noise, quantum indeterminacy. Unpredictability outside is freedom felt inside.
* **Purchasing Predictability by Erasing the Individual**: Because an observer cannot predict a single free variable, they aggregate a population (N -> infinity). By dividing by sqrt(N), individual variance is smoothed away. Out comes a smooth, predictable curve (the Ideal Gas Law, actuarial tables, supply-demand curves). The very predictivity of statistics comes from ignoring individual unpredictability.
* **The Observer's Projection Scam**: The deterministic feeling of the observer is not an objective description of the object; it is the observer's own internal demand for coherence projected onto the aggregated data. Then, classical determinism commits the ultimate theft: it uses the statistical curve to declare that the living, first-person agency that produced it is a mere illusion.
* **The Irreversible Step (+1)**: Macro equations cannot dictate the choice made at the living causal frontier. Reality terminates at the first-person irreversible physical intervention (+1).

### [Intro Script - Mingli]
Think about how we usually argue about determinism versus free will. We’re told that either the universe is a clockwork machine and our choices are an illusion, or the universe is fundamentally random like quantum dice. But look at what actually happens when you trace the perspectives. From the inside of your own mind, you are one hundred percent internally coherent. You know your reasons, you feel your perceptions, and that certainty gives you control—which is the very essence of free choice. But the moment someone looks at you from the outside without knowing your thoughts, your action looks completely unpredictable. And because an observer can’t predict a single free variable, they aggregate millions of them together. They smooth away the individual choices, get a neat statistical curve, and then declare: "Look, the universe is deterministic!"
