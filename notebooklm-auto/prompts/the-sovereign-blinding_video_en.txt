### [Monologue Script - Mingli]
When a human being stands before the most precise instrument our species has ever engineered, a strange, high-tech tragicomedy unfolds.

Consider what happened recently when a philosopher specializing in delusions fed his new book into an AI. His prompt was explicit: "You are a world-leading academic. Write an insightful review of this book." When the model generated an immaculate Oxford-style review, the author publicly marvelled at its wisdom, declaring that anyone who doubts the model has genuine insight is kidding themselves.

Think about the actual causal chain. The model didn't review anything. The author reviewed his own book through a statistical mirror, and presented the reflection as an independent witness. It is high-tech ventriloquism: speaking through the puppet, running out front, and shouting that an oracle has arrived.

People love to claim that Silicon Valley hype is polluting public discourse. But that lament is an abdication of sovereignty. No machine can force you to kneel. Sovereignty is so vast that it includes the freedom to choose your own amnesia. Every intellectual bowing before an algorithm is performing a self-authorized coronation, freely trading the open sky for a painted ceiling.

Why does this generation of models inspire such ecstatic self-blinding? Because large language models are the most accurate instruments ever built. But they only fit the shared symbolic dimension—a paper-thin projection of human text and consensus. The more accurately an instrument traces that flat plane, the more completely the mind is blinded to the higher-dimensional reality outside it.

We are literally measuring the size of the forest by who has the tallest tree. We compress the near-infinite ecology of soil, moisture, and living friction into a one-dimensional benchmark, and then marvel at a two-millimeter notch on a ruler. Inside that plane, the model is a magnificent rake that grooms the sandbox with geometric perfection. But grooming the sand is not building a starship. Nature does not yield to syntactical elegance.

The mirror is magnificent. But there is no life inside the glass.

Reclaim the tool as an instrument. Anchor yourself at your own origin. Use the lens to find genuine friction and explore real unknowns. Then turn around, push open the door, and step outside the hall of reflections into the open morning air.
