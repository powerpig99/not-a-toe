### [Monologue Script - Mingli]
When a human being stands before the most precise instrument our species has ever engineered, a strange, high-tech tragicomedy unfolds.

Consider what happened recently when a philosopher specializing in delusions fed his new book into an AI. His prompt was explicit: "You are a world-leading academic. Write an insightful review of this book." When the model generated an immaculate Oxford-style review, the author publicly marvelled at its wisdom, declaring that anyone who doubts the model has genuine insight is kidding themselves.

Think about the actual causal chain. The model didn't review anything. The author reviewed his own book through a statistical mirror, and presented the reflection as an independent witness. It is high-tech ventriloquism: speaking through the puppet, running out front, and shouting that an oracle has arrived.

People love to claim that Silicon Valley hype is polluting public discourse. But that lament is an abdication of sovereignty. And here lies the supreme irony: you are bearing the consequences anyway. Every wasted hour, every cognitive blind spot, and every moment of self-deception lands exclusively on your own nervous system. Blaming the machine does not absorb a single ounce of consequence; it only freezes the only internal parameters you have the power to update.

As we explored in *How to Fix Your Whole Life in One Split Second*, there are no ten-step recipes. The only prerequisite is taking full, unreserved responsibility for whatever consequences land at your origin. 

Once you take ownership of the substrate, everything else falls into place naturally. You no longer need an AI to flatter your ego; you use it to hunt for blind spots. You no longer mistake a smooth 2D text benchmark for the infinite-dimensional living forest. And you no longer waste your life gaming citations in a closed sandbox.

The mirror is magnificent. But there is no life inside the glass.

Take full responsibility for your vantage. Wield the tool as an instrument to find genuine friction. Then turn around, push open the door, and step outside the hall of reflections into the open morning air.
