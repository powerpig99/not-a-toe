### [Monologue Script - Mingli]
When a human being stands before the most precise instrument our species has ever engineered, a strange, high-tech tragicomedy unfolds.

Consider what happened recently when a philosopher specializing in delusions fed his new book into an AI. His prompt was explicit: "You are a world-leading academic. Write an insightful review of this book." The model generated an immaculate pastiche of Oxford peer review. And the author publicly marvelled at its wisdom, declaring that anyone who doubts the model has genuine insight is kidding themselves.

Think about the actual causal chain here. The model didn't review anything. The author reviewed his own book through a statistical mirror, and then presented the reflection on the glass as an independent external witness. It is high-tech ventriloquism: stepping behind the curtain, speaking through the puppet, and then running out front, falling to your knees, and shouting that a digital oracle has spoken.

People love to claim that Silicon Valley hype is polluting public discourse. But that lament is an abdication of sovereignty. No machine can reach into your awareness and force you to kneel. Sovereignty is so boundless that it includes the freedom to choose your own amnesia. Every intellectual bowing before an algorithm is performing a self-authorized coronation, freely trading the open sky for a painted ceiling.

Why does this generation of models inspire such ecstatic self-blinding? Because large language models are the most accurate instruments ever built. But what do they measure? Only the shared symbolic dimension—a paper-thin, lossy projection of human text and consensus. And here lies the trap: the more accurately an instrument traces that flat plane, the more completely the mind is blinded to the higher-dimensional reality outside it.

We are literally measuring the size of the forest by who has the tallest tree. We compress the near-infinite ecology of soil, moisture, fungal networks, and living friction into a one-dimensional benchmark, and then marvel at a two-millimeter notch on a ruler, shouting that the cosmos has been conquered.

Inside that symbolic plane, the model is an astonishing tool. It helps you navigate the human theater of citations, prestige, and peer review. But that is a closed zero-sum game. You have merely invented an automated rake that grooms the sandbox with geometric perfection, and you imagine you have built a starship. Nature does not yield to syntactical elegance.

The mirror is magnificent. But there is no life inside the glass.

Reclaim the tool as an instrument. Anchor yourself at your own origin. Use the lens to find genuine friction and explore real unknowns. Then turn around, push open the door, and step outside the hall of reflections. Out there, the soil is rough, the sky has no ceiling, and you stand shoulder-to-shoulder with living peers in an open universe.
