# The Reversal from Defensible Claim to Dogma

*A conditional finding on a chosen risk curve freezes as absolute when one metric is held as the whole of safety.*

Large epidemiological analyses show that under specific risk metrics — relative risk of disease incidence, disability-adjusted life years, all-cause mortality — expected harm rises from the first drink, so zero consumption is the lowest measured point on those particular curves. Inside the model, the assumptions, and the chosen metric, that statement is scientifically defensible: provisional, quantitative, open to revision. Public communication rarely leaves it there. The continuous dose–response flattens into a binary absolute; the particular metric is treated as if it exhausted safety; preference, culture, age, health status, and trade-offs between quality and quantity of life drop out. What began as a conditional finding is presented as a settled conclusion that already dictates the only rational or moral choice — abstinence. At that point the communication has reversed: a defensible scientific claim has been turned into an overclaim that operates like dogma. The cost is real — when people notice the missing dimensions, they discount not only the overclaim but the underlying data. The remedy is not to abandon the residue. It is to keep the layers distinct: report what the chosen metric shows under its stated assumptions; leave translation into personal or collective decisions explicitly separate from the science itself.

Scoring all of them through a single epidemiological curve is Capacity failure, not completion of knowledge. ## Overclaim discounts the data with it

When people notice the missing dimensions — when they realize that “safe” is not one consensus concept and that life involves multiple goods that do not collapse into one epidemiological curve — they begin to discount not only the overclaim but the underlying data as well. Trust erodes. Science is made to look like a set of propositions that must be affirmed rather than re-opened under contact. That cost is structural. Collapse the metric into moral absolute, and resistance to the absolute arrives as resistance to the metric. [Openness is consistency](../openness-is-consistency/) names the demand that produced the surface contradiction: force a finite hold to ground what only continues, and contradiction multiplies. The contradiction is not in the curves. It is in the demand that the curves seal the field of personal and collective choice.

The honest trade-off formulation remains available. A center can hold that every drink moves expected harm on certain endpoints and still choose the drink because other goods score higher under that locus’s valuation — the same structure as knowing a dense meal is not optimal for a single metabolic metric and eating it anyway. Clear data. Explicit cost–benefit. No forced sharing of the valuation coordinate. That formulation keeps the residue usable. The absolute formulation spends the residue on a freeze.

## Keep the layers distinct

Dissolution is not a rival absolute — not “moderate drinking is safe,” not a new sealed suite under a different purity metric. Replacing one exhaustive coordinate with another is the same collapse under a new label. Dissolution is restoring axes: report what the chosen metric shows under its stated assumptions and limits; make the model’s bounds visible; leave translation into personal or collective decisions as judgment, preference, and trade-off at the centers that live the consequences.

Judgment words still compress a differential into a heading. What drops is the forced identity of metric and life. The first step is empirical and revisable. Maintaining the distinction is what keeps scientific communication scientific — residue available for re-tracing, not a monument that must be affirmed.
